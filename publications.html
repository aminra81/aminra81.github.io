Maverick: Private and Verifiable LLM Inference Made Practical via Matrix-Vector Multiplication Delegation ↗

Ben Merbaum*, Mohammad Amin Raeisi*, Wenhao Wang, Charalampos Papamanthou, Katerina Sotiraki, Fan Zhang

* Co-first authors, alphabetical order.

Paper PDF↗ (opens in a new tab)Publication↗ (opens in a new tab)BibTeX ↓
Abstract summary

Maverick enables a resource-limited client to delegate the expensive matrix–vector operations in large language model inference while protecting inputs and checking results. Its verification protocol offers information-theoretic soundness, transparent preprocessing, efficient batch checks, and negligible added server work. LPN-based pseudorandom masks provide input privacy. An end-to-end Qwen3-4B prototype measures the resulting throughput gains across client configurations. With one client thread, the reported speedups over local inference reach 17× with online mask generation, 45× with precomputed masks, and 44× for verification alone.

TRIP: Coercion-resistant Registration for E-Voting with Verifiability and Usability in Votegral ↗

Louis-Henri Merino, Simone Colombo, Rene Reyes, Alaleh Azhir, Shailesh Mishra, Pasindu Tennage, Mohammad Amin Raeisi, Haoqian Zhang, Jeff R. Allen, Bernhard Tellenbach, Vero Estrada-Galiñanes, Bryan Ford

Paper PDF↗ (opens in a new tab)Publication↗ (opens in a new tab)BibTeX ↓
Abstract summary

TRIP is the registration component of Votegral, an online voting architecture designed to resist coercion while remaining verifiable and usable. During in-person registration, a voter privately obtains genuine and fake paper credentials from a kiosk. The voter can distinguish them inside the booth, while a coercer cannot distinguish them afterward. Interactive zero-knowledge proofs support credential verification without requiring voters to understand the cryptography. The prototype, usability study, and formal analysis examine practicality, verifiability, and coercion resistance; credential issuance takes at most 19.7 seconds on the tested resource-constrained kiosks.

New Parallel and Streaming Algorithms for Directed Densest Subgraph ↗

Slobodan Mitrović*, Theodore Pan*, Mahdi Qaempanah*, Mohammad Amin Raeisi*

* Equal contribution, alphabetical order.

Paper PDF↗ (opens in a new tab)Publication↗ (opens in a new tab)BibTeX ↓
Abstract summary

This work develops approximation algorithms for finding dense regions of large directed graphs under parallel and streaming memory constraints. In the massively parallel model, it obtains a (2 + ε)-approximation in roughly √log n rounds using sublinear memory on each machine. In the semi-streaming model, a deterministic single-pass algorithm achieves an O(log n)-approximation and also gives an insertion-only dynamic algorithm with O(log² n) worst-case update time. Experiments show stronger practical approximation quality than the worst-case streaming bound and fewer parallel rounds than previous approaches.

Building a BBB Pseudorandom Permutation using Lai-Massey Networks ↗

Ritam Bhaumik*, Mohammad Amin Raeisi*

* Co-first authors, alphabetical order.

Paper PDF↗ (opens in a new tab)Publication↗ (opens in a new tab)BibTeX ↓
Abstract summary

Lai–Massey networks are a classical block-cipher construction whose provable security has received less attention than Feistel and substitution–permutation designs. This paper studies their beyond-birthday-bound pseudorandom-permutation security when round functions are independent and random, against chosen-plaintext attacks. It establishes a tight round threshold: five rounds suffice for security beyond the birthday bound, and fewer rounds do not.
